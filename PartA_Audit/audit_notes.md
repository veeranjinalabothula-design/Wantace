# Part A — Operational audit notes

> **Status:** Sample audit framework, not the record of a verified site visit. Confirm each observation with Vardhan Steelware staff before treating it as fact.

## Objective

Assess how a small steelware business could record, track, and communicate customer orders from receipt through dispatch and returns.

## Illustrative current-state assumptions

The following are discovery prompts, not confirmed findings:

- Orders may arrive through calls, messages, or in-person discussions.
- A paper register or spreadsheet may be the current record of party, item, quantity, rate, and remarks.
- Dispatch updates may be recorded separately from the original order.
- Staff may need a shared, searchable view to answer order-status questions.

## Risks to validate

| Area | Question / possible risk | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order intake | Are important fields missed when an order is received verbally? | Observe intake; compare recent orders with invoices |
| Duplicate records | Can staff identify repeat entries for the same party and item? | Review a sample of dated register entries |
| Status tracking | Is it clear who changed an order to dispatched, hold, or return? | Walk through status updates with staff |
| Data quality | Are quantity units, rates, and party names entered consistently? | Review anonymized sample records |
| Access and backup | Who can access records, and how are backups made? | Confirm current device, account, and backup practices |
| Customer privacy | Are phone numbers or payment details exposed unnecessarily? | Review actual collection and sharing practices |

## Proposed pilot controls

1. Capture party, item, quantity, unit, rate, status, and optional remarks in one record.
2. Require core fields and validate quantity/rate before saving.
3. Use a consistent status vocabulary: pending, dispatched, hold, return.
4. Limit access to authorized staff; agree a backup and retention procedure before production use.
5. Review a small pilot weekly and log corrections rather than overwriting paper records without approval.
